**SLIDE 1 — COVER (0:15, Sophie)**

"Hi — we're Campus Call. I'm Sophie, this is Nico. This weekend we built a live forecast board for the questions this campus actually argues about — and a way for the community to win when the crowd calls it right."

**SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM (0:35, Sophie)**

"Here's a morning every Mustang knows. It's 7:40. The 4A is due at Kennedy Library at 7:48. Will it make your 8 a.m.? The group chat has opinions. A storm's rolling in — will farmers' market even happen Thursday? More opinions. Campus runs on these little predictions all day long, and nobody ever writes the number down. Nobody keeps score. And the platforms that do keep score — Polymarket, Kalshi — will never list your bus. The local event is unowned."

**SLIDE 3 — KNOWLEDGE, NOT A GAME (0:50, Nico)**

"Now — you might look at prediction markets and see gambling. We see knowledge. Federal Reserve researchers found prediction markets beat Wall Street economists on inflation — twenty-two percent lower error, and never significantly worse on anything they tested. An independent analysis of Polymarket's resolved markets found the crowd pointed at the right outcome ninety percent of the time, a full month early. And that knowledge has a price. Companies spend a hundred fifty-three billion dollars a year on market research, and the owner of the New York Stock Exchange just put two billion into Polymarket — explicitly to distribute its event data. Nobody produces that signal for local questions. We do."

**SLIDE 4 — THE SOLUTION (0:35, Nico)**

"Campus Call is a live forecast board for your campus. Free credits, real prizes, zero cash. Every market is a real local question — the 7:48 bus, the storm, tonight's game. Every market opens at a researched probability, never a coin flip. Every market names a written rule and the public source that settles it — so it's auditable, not vibes. And every sponsored market names a community beneficiary, up front."

**SLIDE 5 — WHY NOW (0:25, Nico)**

"Why hasn't this existed? Because local events have no probability feed — no bookmaker will ever price your farmers' market. This year, AI changed that. Our pipeline reads schedules, weather and transit feeds, drafts the question and the rule, prices it from public data, and suggests the result. A human confirms every resolution. Cost per market: basically zero."

**SLIDE 6 — WHO PAYS (0:30, Sophie)**

"And the business model, addressed head-on: players never fund each other. In a real-money market, winners are paid by losers — the house needs you to lose. Here, credits are free, and prizes come from whoever wants the crowd's attention: event organizers — this hackathon is literally customer number one — local sponsors, and the institution, which gets demand forecasts no survey can match. The community is the beneficiary, never the counterparty."

**SLIDE 7 — TRACTION (0:25, Sophie)**

"We didn't just theorize — we launched Saturday. Twenty-nine forecasters, fifty-five tickets, twelve thousand credits in play across forty-five live markets. Our most active market — will Sunday hit seventy degrees — opened at thirty-seven percent, the crowd pushed it to forty-six, and it resolves tonight from the National Weather Service station at the airport."

**SLIDE 8 — WHY US (0:15, Nico)**

"Two of us built this in one weekend — with a coordinated team of Claude agents handling database, UI, research and QA. A live product, forty-five researched markets, a pricing pipeline, real users. That's one weekend. Imagine a quarter."

**SLIDE 9 — CLOSE (0:10, Sophie)**

"Same thrill of calling it — this time, the community wins. Scan the QR, take your thousand credits, and make your first call before the storm hits. We're Campus Call. Thank you."

## Delivery notes

- **"Gambling" is spoken once, on slide 3, as the setup you knock down.** It never appears on a slide, and neither do *bet, wager, odds, payout* — keep the spoken vocabulary to forecasts, tickets, credits, prizes.
- **If pushed on the 90% stat in Q&A:** "That's an independent analysis of Polymarket's resolved markets — how often the favorite won, not calibration. The rigorous source is the Fed paper, which is why we lead with it."
- **If pushed on "who buys a weather forecast you got from a free feed":** "They don't — utility markets are the daily habit and build scored track records; demand markets — attendance, turnout, sellouts — are the data, because the crowd is the only source. Sponsors pay for the attention in between."
- **Pace check:** the script is ~540 words; at 140 wpm that's 3:50. If you're running hot, the cut order is: slide 8's last sentence, then slide 5's last sentence, then the Kalshi sentence on slide 2.
- **Transitions are baked in** (problem ends on "unowned" → knowledge begins "you might look at…"; knowledge ends "we do" → solution begins "Campus Call is…"). Don't ad-lib bridges; they're where time dies.
- Re-pull traction numbers right before presenting if possible; if the live counts have grown, say the bigger number and the slide becomes conservative — never the reverse.
