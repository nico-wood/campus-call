# Incentives — Campus Call

Research note for a free, skill-based forecasting contest inside the Cal Poly AI Hackathon (13–15 November 2026). Campus Call is free to enter. Nothing is purchased. Virtual credits have no cash value. Rank comes from forecasting accuracy (Brier score / points), not a drawing. Words to use: forecast, position, market probability, points. Words not to use: bet, wager, odds, payout.

This note does not post anything, and it does not ask anyone to spam Discord, email, or group chats.

Nico set the prize on 13 November 2026: 50% of what the team actually receives, paid to 10 skill ranks on a geometric list. An earlier decision in this file said 25%. The live rule, the entry path, the tax disclaimer, and the 11:30 a.m. Sunday close are in `research/00-summary.md`. Where this note says 20%, 25%, 50/30/20, a Sunday 3:00 p.m. lock, or three checkpoints, the summary wins. The room tactics below still apply. Do not say the dollars until the summary's gates are met. Do not describe a cash award as tax-free.

## 1. A share of this team’s winnings

The only cash idea on the table is a share of **this team’s** hackathon winnings. It exists only if the team actually receives money, and only if the hackathon rules and any sponsor terms allow the team to share it. They may win nothing. No prize amount is assumed below.

Lock the offer before the first forecast. Do not raise it if the team later wins big, and do not quietly shrink it.

| Share | Fairness to the builders | Attractiveness to forecasters | Awkwardness if this team also runs the markets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10% | Easiest yes inside the team. Almost all of a prize they earned by shipping stays with them. | Weak. “A tenth of a prize we might not win” will not move a tired builder at lunch. | Lowest. The side contest is obviously not why the team built a product. |
| 20% | Builders still keep a majority. “A fifth” is a sentence teammates can agree to without feeling taxed. | The only share worth saying out loud. It is still a maybe, so it is not the reason to join. | Manageable if the team is off the ranked board and says that first. |
| 30% | A teammate who coded all night can fairly object. A third of an unwon prize is a large IOU. | Little better than 20% on Saturday, because the dollar is still unknown and may be zero. | High. Other teams and the judges can read it as the operators taxing the weekend to feed a side contest they administer. |

**Recommend 20%.** Ten percent is so easy to ignore that it is not worth the airtime. Thirty percent buys awkwardness without fixing the real problem, which is that the pool might be zero. Twenty percent is the smallest share that sounds like a commitment and still leaves the builders with most of anything they win.

Say the number only after every builder on the team has agreed and someone has read the prize rules. Until that check is done, the spoken line is “a share, if we win something and the rules allow it,” not “20%.”

**If the team wins nothing, the pool is $0.** The contest still has to be worth entering: a badge in the app and an honest certificate. Those are the real offer. Cash is upside, and the script should say the zero case in the same breath as the share.

## 2. Skill-based leaderboard prizes

Cash, if any, goes to three people. Recognition goes further down so the board is not only a podium.

**Split of the pool (which may be $0): 50% / 30% / 20% for 1st / 2nd / 3rd.** Places 4–10 get no cash. They get a name on the Sunday board, the featured-forecaster badge, and a certificate that states the rank.

Why the tail still shows up: not for money. On Saturday nobody knows whether this team will win a prize, so 4th through 10th are not “almost cash.” They show up because the board is visible in the room, a top-10 line is a real cutoff rather than a PDF for everyone who opened the app, and with a short slate of markets a few good forecasts can move someone onto that line. The team-vs-team board and the three checkpoints (below) give people who are 15th a reason to reopen the app Sunday morning. Referral credits must not be what puts them there.

**Ties: the person who first reached the tied point total ranks higher.** Do not split cash and do not draw lots. Identical scores are likely if there are only a handful of markets, and splitting a maybe-zero pool into fractions is how a clear contest becomes a group chat. Reaching the score sooner is a real forecasting act: you committed before more information arrived. Use the timestamp when that person’s total first equaled the tied score, not the time they created an account. Say this rule in the app before markets open. The cost is real: someone who updated later and landed on the same total ranks lower. That is better than a drawing, and better than negotiating the tie at the table.

**Ineligible, and they do not appear on the ranked board:**

- Campus Call administrators and builders, even if they forecast to test the app. A staff row can exist for the demo. It is not ranked and cannot win.
- Hackathon judges and event organizers. They may know the demo order or have a hand in the outcome.
- Anyone who takes a position on a market about their own project team. That forecast does not score. If the only markets are about projects in the room, those people can still forecast on every market that is not their own.

Publish the ineligible list with the rules, not after someone is winning.

## 3. What still works when the pool is $0

Ranked by how much each one helps a student say yes **on Saturday**, in the room, with a laptop open and a demo to finish.

1. **Featured forecaster badge in the app.** This is the Saturday yes. It shows up next to their name while friends are looking at the same screen. It needs no sponsor and no prize. It can be given for top 10, for completing all three checkpoints, or for both, as long as the labels stay distinct. A badge that everyone receives is not a badge.

2. **A “Top Forecaster” certificate.** Useful, but it pays off on LinkedIn later, not at lunch. It is why a careful student still joins when they do not care about a pixel badge. It is second because a PDF they cannot see yet loses to a board they can see now.

3. **Early access to the next campus.** Weak on Saturday. No next campus is announced. “We might run this somewhere else” is not a reason to stop building. Mention it only to someone who already likes the board. Do not lead with it.

4. **Sponsor swag.** Last, and only as a blank. **Do not promise it.** New Times SLO is a media partner on the registration page, not a donor of objects to Campus Call. No sponsor has been confirmed as giving this team anything to hand out. If a real sponsor physically hands over a finite stack of stickers or shirts, with permission to give them as recognition, swag jumps to second for the table hour only, because an object beats a promise. Until that happens, swag is not part of the ask.

### What the certificate should and should not claim

It should name the person, the rank, and the denominator (“3rd of 27 ranked forecasters” — the real N, including a small N). It should say Campus Call at the Cal Poly AI Hackathon, 13–15 November 2026, ranked by forecasting accuracy (Brier score / points) on the published markets, free to enter, opt-in, virtual credits with no cash value, run by a student team.

It should not claim university credit, a Cal Poly or Orfalea or club endorsement, a job, a professional certification, “winner of the hackathon,” or any dollar figure unless that dollar was actually paid under the rules. It should not imply the hackathon judges ranked them. It should not imply a verified crowd larger than the app count. It should not use bet, wager, odds, or payout.

## 4. Referral credits

**Product rule (assumption):** a referral credit is recorded only when the referred person locks at least one real position on a scored market, using their own account, during the contest. An account with no forecast pays nothing. Self-referral pays nothing. A second account on the same phone does not count. Organizers, judges, and Campus Call staff cannot be the referred person.

**Product rule (assumption):** referral credits never change the accuracy rank. They are a separate tally, capped at **5 qualified referrals**. Referral six and beyond gets a thank-you on screen and zero further credits. Credits are not cash, not convertible, and not a share of the pool.

Why the hard wall: if credits enter the same points as accuracy, a popular person can outrank a better forecaster. That breaks the skill claim the prize depends on. A cap of “10% of points” still lets referrals swing a short weekend slate. Five is enough to reward “text the person across the table” and too small to be a strategy. The growth mechanic is the ask, not the credit. If the team later insists credits touch the score, the cap should be restated before open as no more than the point value of a single market, still max five, and the certificate must say the rank includes that bonus. Default is: they do not touch the rank.

Do not confuse this with the hackathon’s own `?ref=` ambassador links. Those are the organizers’ registration program. Campus Call does not pay, boost, or ride them.

## 5. Checkpoints and forecasting squads

The event window on the calendar is about 53 hours, and the useful forecasting window is shorter: Saturday in the building through Sunday’s demos. Do not pretend this is a daily habit. Do not show a 7-day streak, a flame icon for “day 2,” or a push that says “don’t break your streak” as if it were a consumer app.

**Three checkpoints, badge only, zero points.** Points would let attendance beat accuracy.

Assumed times (not on the calendar; set them in the app by Saturday noon and move them only once if organizers publish a real pitch time):

| Checkpoint | Window (Pacific) | What counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Saturday 12:00pm–6:00pm | One locked position |
| 2 | Saturday 6:00pm–Sunday 11:00am | One new or updated position |
| 3 | Sunday 11:00am until lock | One position still locked at the deadline |

Missing one breaks the set. There is no makeup. The badge is “three calls,” not “daily streak.”

**Lock assumption:** Sunday 3:00pm Pacific, so scoring can finish before the calendar end at 5:00pm. If demos are earlier, move the lock once, in the app, and do not score forecasts made after the posted lock.

**Forecasting squads are not project teams.** A squad is 2–4 people who opt in as a forecasting group. The screen says “forecasting squad,” never “your hackathon team,” so nobody thinks the score is about their build.

Keep it skill-based:

- Squad score is the **mean** accuracy of members, not the sum. A sum rewards recruiting bodies.
- Each member needs at least three scored forecasts or the squad is unranked. Empty seats do not help.
- Any market that names a member’s project is dropped for the whole squad, not only for that member. Sitting out while telling your squad what to do is the conflict this rule is there to block.
- Squads get a name on the screen. They do not get a second cash pool. Cash stays with the individual top 3.
- Campus Call staff cannot be on a ranked squad. Judges and organizers cannot either.

## 8. Proof of real users

Judges should see that strangers used it. Capture these. Do not fabricate, backfill, or count anything soft.

1. **Named count from the app.** Distinct accounts that locked at least one position before the lock, with user id, display name, market id, position, and timestamp. Export the rows. A signup, a QR scan, a Discord reaction, or an account with no forecast is not a user.
2. **The denominator, said plainly.** “27 ranked forecasters, of which 4 are us” is acceptable. “Over 100 participants” is not, unless the export shows 100 accounts that each locked a forecast.
3. **A leaderboard that is not the founders.** The top 10 on the Sunday screen should include people outside the Campus Call team. If it does not, show the true board and say so. Hiding staff rows is required; inventing rival rows is not.
4. **One photo of the room using it**, taken with agreement, during lunch or the Sunday board, not a staged shot of the team holding phones. Store the time it was taken.
5. **A short integrity note for questions.** Staff accounts flagged and unranked. Ineligible forecasts excluded. Referral credits not in the accuracy score. No batch of forecasts written in the last five minutes by the same admin. If a judge asks, open the timestamp list.
6. **What not to capture into the demo.** Email addresses, resumes, payment status, or the organizer’s participant export. Those are not proof, and they are not this team’s list to show.

If the true number is 18, the proof is 18.

## What would make 100 fail

It is a hard goal. The ways it dies are ordinary.

- **The room is smaller than the goal.** Headcount is unpublished. One hundred unique forecasters cannot appear if fewer than one hundred people are there, and people outside the building are the wrong proof.
- **Saturday is for building.** They paid $20 and gave up a weekend to ship a product. A forecasting app is a distraction. If the first session takes more than two minutes, or the first three people hit an error on Building 003 Wi-Fi, the table is done.
- **The cash pitch is a mirage.** Leading with a share of a prize this team has not won, and might be forbidden to share, sounds like a scheme. The vocabulary rules exist so it does not sound like a book. Even said cleanly, “maybe money Sunday” loses to “finish our demo.”
- **Distribution is a favor.** No mic, no screen, no table means friends of a four-person team. That is often 15 to 25 people. One Discord post will be seen by whoever is scrolling, then buried.
- **The trust problem is structural.** This team competes in the hackathon and administers the markets. Ineligibility has to be visible or people will assume the board is for the operators. Markets about “which project wins” make that worse.
- **The eligible pool shrinks itself.** Judges, organizers, staff, an 18+ gate if adopted, and anyone blocked off markets about their own team all come out of an unknown room.
- **Padding.** Counting scans, staff accounts, or the same person twice will not survive a question. Chasing 100 after Saturday 6:00pm is how that temptation shows up. The checkpoint is there to stop it.

## Top 5 takeaways

1. Do not plan on a crowd size or a prize figure. Neither is on the calendar or the registration page. One hundred may be most of the room. The working goal is 40 real forecasts, and under 15 by Saturday 6:00pm means 100 is already dead.
2. Commit 20% of this team’s winnings only, and only with a unanimous teammate yes plus a rules check. The pool is $0 if they win nothing. Badge and certificate are the product; cash is a sentence you can delete.
3. Pay the top 3 at 50/30/20 if there is money. Places 4–10 are recognition. Ties go to whoever reached the score first. Staff, judges, organizers, and anyone positioned on their own project are off the board.
4. Referral credits are capped at 5 and do not affect accuracy rank. The “streak” is three checkpoints over the weekend, badge only. Squads are forecasting groups scored by mean accuracy, and they skip any market about a member’s project.
5. One table, one short speech per willing workshop, one screen demo if allowed, one Discord post. Proof is an export of named forecasts, a board that is not the founders, and a consented photo. If the number is small, show the small number.
